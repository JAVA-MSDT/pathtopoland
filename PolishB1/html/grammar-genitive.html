Step 3: Dopełniacz (Genitive Case)

The most heavily tested case on the B1 exam. Essential for negation, quantities, possession, and location — it appears in every single subtest. Master this after Instrumental.

Step 3 of 7 Most Tested Negation Quantities

Why This Is the Most Important Case

Genitive is unavoidable — it appears in all five subtests

No other case is as pervasive as Genitive. Here is where you will encounter it on exam day:

  • Grammar subtest: Negation drills are almost always present. Transforming a positive sentence into a negative one requires Genitive — always.
  • Writing subtest: Formal complaints and informal letters both rely on Genitive for expressing possession (auto mojego szefa), purpose (dla ciebie), and quantities (dużo czasu).
  • Reading subtest: Passages are written in natural Polish — Genitive appears in every paragraph. Recognising it passively helps you decode meaning faster under time pressure.
  • Listening subtest: Speakers use negation, quantities, and location prepositions constantly. Knowing Genitive forms lets you catch key facts.
  • Speaking subtest: You will need szukam pracy (I'm looking for work), potrzebuję pomocy (I need help), and similar Genitive structures in role-play scenarios.
⚠️ The 51% rule applies per subtest. Genitive errors in the Grammar subtest cascade into the Writing subtest score. Fixing this one case can be the difference between passing and failing.

Trigger 1 — Negation (nie + verb)

Whenever a positive sentence becomes negative, the direct object shifts from Accusative to Genitive. This is the most tested transformation on the exam.

  • Mam czas (Accusative) → Nie mam czasu (Genitive)
  • Kupuję kawę (Accusative) → Nie kupuję kawy (Genitive)
  • Widzę brata (Accusative) → Nie widzę brata (Genitive)
  • Jest tu mleko → Nie ma tu mleka — note: nie ma always takes Genitive

Trigger 2 — Quantities and Numbers

Words expressing amount or quantity always govern Genitive: dużo, mało, trochę, kilka, wiele, ile, dość. Numbers from 5 upward also take Genitive plural.

  • dużo czasu — a lot of time
  • mało pieniędzy — little money
  • kilka osób — a few people
  • pięć godzin — five hours

Trigger 3 — Possession

To show that something belongs to someone, place the owner in Genitive after the possessed object. This is the Polish equivalent of the English apostrophe-s.

  • auto mojego szefa — my boss's car
  • dom mojej mamy — my mother's house
  • numer telefonu kolegi — a colleague's phone number

Trigger 4 — Prepositions of Location and Direction

Several common prepositions always require Genitive. Do, z, od, dla, bez, obok, oprócz, podczas, u, blisko, koło, naprzeciwko all take Genitive.

  • Idę do sklepu. — I am going to the shop.
  • Wracam z pracy. — I am coming back from work.
  • Kawa bez cukru. — Coffee without sugar.

Ending Rules

Genitive endings at a glance

Unlike Instrumental (almost perfectly regular), Genitive masculine endings require some word-by-word memorisation. The good news: feminine endings are very consistent.

Category Ending Base → Genitive Notes
Feminine singular -y or -i kawa → kawy · muzyka → muzyki Use -y after hard consonants; -i after k, g, and soft consonants
Masculine Animate ♂🧑
(persons, animals)		 -a always brat → brata · lekarz → lekarza · pies → psa Animate masculine ALWAYS takes -a — consistent rule, no exceptions
Masculine Inanimate ♂📦
(objects — concrete pattern)		 -a klucz → klucza · nóż → noża · słownik → słownika Many concrete objects take -a — but not all; must be learnt per word
Masculine Inanimate ♂📦
(objects — building/abstract pattern)		 -u dom → domu · czas → czasu · autobus → autobusu Buildings, abstracts, and mass nouns often take -u — must be learnt per word
Neuter singular -a mleko → mleka · miasto → miasta Neuter is very regular: always -a
Plural (all genders) -ów / -i / -y / Ø dom → domów · kobieta → kobiet Plural Genitive has the most variation — learn high-frequency forms with their plurals
Shortcut for feminine nouns: If the feminine noun ends in -ka, it usually becomes -ki in Genitive (kawa → kawy is an exception — kawa ends in -wa, not -ka). For nouns ending in -ka: herbata → herbaty, muzyka → muzyki, praca → pracy. When in doubt, say the word and ask "does it sound harsh?" — if yes, use -y; if the consonant is k/g, use -i.

Adjective Agreement in Genitive (Zgodność Przymiotnika)

How adjectives change — gender agreement in Genitive

In the Genitive case, adjectives must agree with the noun's gender. Animacy does NOT affect adjective endings in Genitive — masculine animate (people, animals) and masculine inanimate (objects) use exactly the same adjective form.

⭐ Key contrast with Accusative: In the Accusative, masculine animate gets a different adjective ending (dobrego) while inanimate stays at dobry. In Genitive, both animate and inanimate masculine nouns use the same adjective ending -ego. Animacy only splits adjectives in Accusative.
Noun Type Adjective Ending Nominative Phrase Genitive Phrase Example Sentence
Masculine Inanimate ♂📦
(object)		 -ego / -iego dobry czas dobrego czasu Nie mam dobrego czasu.
Masculine Animate ♂🧑
(person / animal)		 -ego / -iego
Same as inanimate!		 dobry brat dobrego brata Nie widzę dobrego brata.
Feminine ♀ -ej / -iej dobra praca dobrej pracy Szukam dobrej pracy.
Neuter ⬜ -ego / -iego
(same as masculine)		 dobre mleko dobrego mleka Nie kupuję dobrego mleka.
Plural (all genders) -ych / -ich dobre plany dobrych planów Nie mam dobrych planów.
The two-rule system: Masculine and Neuter adjectives both use -ego/-iego in Genitive — they look the same. Feminine adjectives use -ej/-iej. The entire adjective system in Genitive reduces to: masculine/neuter → dobrego, feminine → dobrej.
Negation with adjective — full Genitive phrase:
"Nie mam dobrego samochodu." — masculine inanimate (adj dobry → dobrego)
"Nie widzę dobrego lekarza." — masculine animate (adj dobry → dobrego — same form!)
"Nie mam dobrej pracy." — feminine (adj dobra → dobrej)
"Nie piję zimnego mleka." — neuter (adj zimny → zimnego)

6 Verbs That Always Take Genitive

These verbs require Genitive even in positive (non-negated) sentences

Unlike the negation rule (which is automatic), these verbs take Genitive regardless of polarity. Memorise them as a set.

Verb (Infinitive) Meaning Example Sentence Exam Context

Critical distinction: z + Genitive = "from" (direction / origin). z + Instrumental = "with" (accompaniment). "Wracam z pracy" (Genitive — from work) vs. "Idę z kolegą" (Instrumental — with a colleague). The meaning of z tells you which case to use.

Ćwiczenia

✏️ Ćwiczenie — Dopełniacz

Wpisz słowo w nawiasie w formie dopełniacza. Spróbuj odpowiedzieć przed sprawdzeniem.

1. Nie mam ______ (czas).

czasu — Masculine abstract noun, Genitive ending -u. czas → czasu. Triggered by nie mam (negation).

2. Szukam ______ (praca).

pracy — Feminine noun, Genitive ending -y (after c → soft). praca → pracy. Triggered by the verb szukać (always Genitive).

3. Piję ______ (kawa) bez ______ (cukier).

kawę / cukru — Note: kawę is Accusative (positive sentence, direct object — no negation). cukru is Genitive (masculine noun after preposition bez, ending -u: cukier → cukru). Two different cases in one sentence!

4. Idę do ______ (sklep).

sklepu — Masculine noun, Genitive ending -u. sklep → sklepu. Triggered by preposition do (always Genitive).

5. Nie widzę ______ (pies).

psa — Masculine animate noun, Genitive ending -a. pies → psa (stem change: ie → Ø). Triggered by nie widzę (negation).
