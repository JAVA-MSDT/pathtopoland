Subtest 1: Listening (Słuchanie)

Your ear for Polish will be tested on everyday dialogues, public announcements, and radio-style segments. Learn the exam structure and use the strategies on this page to reach the 51% threshold every time.

Subtest 1 of 5 ~30 Minutes 51% Pass Threshold Multiple Choice

What the Listening Subtest Looks Like

Exam Snapshot — Subtest 1: The Listening subtest runs for approximately 30 minutes. You listen to a series of short and medium-length recordings — everyday dialogues, public announcements, voicemails, and brief news or radio segments — and answer questions about each one. Task formats include multiple choice (choose one correct answer from A/B/C) and matching (match a recording to its topic or context). Most recordings are played twice. You cannot pause or rewind, so preparation strategy is everything.
Format detailWhat to expect
Duration~30 minutes (including preparation time for each task)
Number of tasks3 task blocks, 10–15 individual questions in total
Audio playsTwice per recording (use the first play for main idea, second for details)
Language of recordingsStandard Warsaw-accent Polish at natural conversational speed
Pass threshold51% — you must score at least half of the available points

What the Examiners Are Testing

Four skill areas — every question maps to one of these

Understanding which skill a question targets helps you know exactly what to listen for — and what to ignore.

1. Main Idea Comprehension

Many questions ask what a conversation or broadcast is about — the topic, purpose, or general message. You do not need to understand every word; you need to identify the central theme. Practice listening to Polish radio or TV for 5 minutes and immediately stating — in one sentence — what the segment was about.

2. Specific Detail Extraction

These questions ask for concrete facts: a price, a time, a person's name, a location, a date. The answer is usually a single piece of information said clearly in the audio. The trap is that distractors will mention similar-sounding numbers or related words. Write the detail down the moment you hear it — do not rely on memory at the end of the recording.

3. Inference from Context

Some questions test whether you can infer a speaker's attitude, intention, or the implicit outcome of a conversation. The word "infer" means the answer is not stated outright — you must read tone, word choice, and context. Example: a speaker saying "Spróbuję" (I'll try) is not the same as a firm agreement. Notice hedging language.

4. Announcement-Type Polish

Public transport announcements, store broadcasts, school PA systems, and radio news follow predictable formulas. If you learn the standard phrases (see the vocabulary table below), you can predict the structure of the message before you hear all of it — dramatically improving your comprehension speed under exam pressure.

Strategy Guide — 5 Concrete Tips

Use these on every listening task, in order

Tip 1 — Read all questions BEFORE the audio starts. You are given preparation time before each task block. Use every second of it. Read every question stem and every answer option. Underline the content word in each option — a name, a number, a verb, a location. Your brain will subconsciously filter the audio for those exact patterns as you listen.
Tip 2 — Focus on the question topic; ignore irrelevant details. Exam recordings often contain extra information not tested in any question. This is intentional — it is designed to distract you. Once you know what each question is about from your pre-read, you can filter out the noise and lock in on the relevant segment of the audio.
Tip 3 — Note numbers, names, and locations as the audio plays. Detail questions (see skill area 2) always require a specific fact. Write on your question paper the moment you hear a number or name, even if you are not sure which question it belongs to. Matching your notes to questions is faster and more reliable than relying on working memory.
Tip 4 — Use process of elimination on distractors. Polish B1 listening distractors are plausible — they use real Polish words related to the topic. When you are uncertain, eliminate options you are sure are wrong rather than trying to find the perfect answer. Two eliminations from three options gives you a 50/50 chance; that is better than a random guess.
Tip 5 — Trust your first instinct; do not overthink. If you clearly heard information that matches one option on the first play, write it down and move on. The most common cause of a correct answer being changed to a wrong one is second-guessing. The second play is for verification and catching gaps — not for reconsidering everything.

Types of Listening Texts at B1 Level

What you will hear and what to listen for

Text Type Setting What to Listen For Common Traps
Dialogue (2 people talking) Everyday situation — café, office, home, shop, doctor's surgery Topic of conversation, whether the speakers agree or disagree, any plans or decisions made Similar words with different meanings; a speaker changing their mind mid-conversation
Public announcement Train or bus station, school, shopping centre, airport What action is required (e.g. go to, wait at, avoid), the time or platform, the location mentioned Numbers and dates stated quickly; the announcement may mention two platform numbers — only one is relevant
Radio / TV segment News bulletin, weather report, traffic update, cultural programme Key facts in sequence (what happened, where, when), the main conclusion or recommendation Distractor details — the presenter may mention related statistics or background information not tested in any question
Phone conversation Customer service call, booking inquiry, appointment scheduling The purpose of the call, whether the request was fulfilled, any conditions or limitations mentioned Polite refusals that sound like confirmations ("Spróbujemy", "Zobaczymy") versus definite agreements ("Oczywiście", "Na pewno")
Voicemail / recorded message Automated system, missed-call message, reminder notification Caller's name, the reason for the call, any action the listener should take, times or deadlines Numbers spoken quickly; phone numbers that sound similar to dates or times in the same recording

The 3 Recording Types on the Exam

Know the structure before exam day — it removes all surprises

The Listening subtest always contains the same three task blocks in roughly the same order. Knowing what each block looks like means you can prepare your listening stance before the first word is spoken.

Recording Type What You Hear What You Must Answer
Short monologues (5–7 clips) Announcements, voicemails, weather reports, radio ads — each 20–40 seconds long Match each clip to its topic or context from a provided list of options
Two-person dialogue (1–2 recordings) Everyday conversations — at the doctor's, booking a hotel room, discussing weekend plans, a disagreement between flatmates True / False / Not Stated questions, or multiple choice about the main points of the conversation
Longer monologue or interview (1 recording) Radio interview, public lecture, or news segment — typically 2–3 minutes long Fill in missing information in provided sentences, or multiple choice questions about specific details
⚠️ The recording plays twice. First play: grasp the main idea and confirm which question is about which part of the audio. Second play: target the specific details needed to answer each question. Do not write full answers on the first play — save that for the second.

The 3-Step Listening Method

Apply this sequence to every task block — without exception

  1. Pre-listen — questions first (use all preparation time): Before the audio starts, read every question and underline the key content word in each answer option — a name, a number, an activity, a location. This primes your attention. You are not reading to understand; you are loading search targets into your working memory.
  2. First listen — main ideas only: Do not panic if you miss individual words. Focus on WHO is speaking, WHAT is happening, and WHY. The recording plays twice — save detail-hunting for the second play. After the first play, write a quick note of what you are confident about and mark uncertain questions clearly.
  3. Second listen — specific details: Now that you know the context, go hunting for the underlined keywords. Write your answer the moment you hear the information — do not wait for the recording to end. If you confirmed an answer on the first play, use the second play to check it and move attention to unsolved questions.

Vocabulary — Common Announcement Phrases

Learn these formulas — they appear in almost every exam

Polish public announcements follow strict templates. Recognising the opening phrase tells you immediately what type of information will follow, and you can prepare to catch the specific detail (platform number, time, action required).

Polish Phrase English Meaning What Follows in the Announcement
Uwaga pasażerowie… Attention passengers… A change to a train/bus service — listen for a platform number, departure time, or cancellation
Pociąg do… odjeżdża z peronu… The train to… departs from platform… Destination city and platform number — these are the testable details
Ze względu na… Due to… / Because of… A reason for a delay, change, or closure — the cause is testable; note it immediately
Proszę o… Please… (formal request) An action the listener must take — go somewhere, wait, present a document, leave an area
Zapraszamy do… We invite you to… / You are welcome at… A promotion, event, or location — often used in shop announcements; note where or what is being promoted
Informujemy, że… We inform you that… An official notice — the clause following że is the testable information; the reason or action follows immediately
W związku z… In connection with… / As a result of… Links a cause to a consequence — both parts may be tested separately
Uprzejmie prosimy… We kindly ask… (very formal) A formal instruction — the verb immediately after is the action required from the listener
Quick practice technique: Before exam week, turn on Polish national radio (polskieradio.pl) or search YouTube for "polskie ogłoszenia stacyjne" (Polish station announcements). Listen only for the opening phrase — pause after it and predict what information will follow. Then play the rest and check whether your prediction was right. This builds the automatic pattern recognition that the exam relies on.

Week 10 Practice Plan — Listening Focus

Goal: complete 3 full past-exam listening subtests in 7 days

Target: Build the habit of identifying main ideas and specific details from natural-speed Polish speech within the 30-minute window. All materials listed below are free.

Day Activity Time
Day 64 Download past exam audio Set 1 from certyfikatpolski.pl. Read all questions first (5 min). Listen once — answer. Listen again — adjust answers. Score with the official answer key. 40 min
Day 65 Re-listen to Day 64 recordings. This time, transcribe the short monologues word for word. Every word you cannot write is a vocabulary gap — add those words to your Anki deck immediately. 30 min
Day 66 Listen to 10 minutes of Polskie Radio news live or on demand. Write down 10 words you recognise and 5 you did not understand. Look up the 5 unknown words and add them to Anki. 25 min
Day 67 Download past exam audio Set 2. Repeat the Day 64 approach exactly. After scoring, compare with Day 64 — identify which task type (monologues / dialogue / long interview) is still your weakest. 40 min
Day 68 YouTube: search "słuchanie certyfikat B1 ćwiczenia". Watch a Polish mock listening walkthrough. After each question, pause the video and answer before the teacher explains. This forces active processing. 30 min
Day 69 Download past exam audio Set 3. Full timed attempt under exam conditions — no pausing, no rewinding. Score your result. Focus all remaining study time this week on whichever task type scored lowest. 40 min
Day 70 Vocabulary catch-up: review all Anki words added this week from the recordings. These are exactly the words appearing at B1 level in real exam audio. Cards you still miss go to the front of the review queue. 20 min
⚠️ The single most important listening exercise you can do is a real past-exam audio file played at normal speed without pausing — not slowed-down clips, not video subtitles, not podcasts designed for learners. The exam uses standard native-speaker speed. Only practice at that speed prepares you for it.

Ćwiczenia Praktyczne — Rozumienie ze słuchu

Ćwiczenie 1 — Prawda czy fałsz?

Instrukcja: Proszę przeczytać zapis ogłoszenia stacyjnego i zaznaczyć, czy podane zdania są prawdziwe (P) czy fałszywe (F). W prawdziwym egzaminie usłyszysz nagranie audio — tutaj zastępuje je tekst pisany.

Tekst ogłoszenia (zapis nagrania):

„Uwaga pasażerowie! Pociąg pospieszny numer 1234 relacji Warszawa–Kraków odjeżdża dziś z peronu czwartego o godzinie czternastej trzydzieści pięć. Ze względu na remont infrastruktury, peron trzeci jest chwilowo nieczynny. Przepraszamy za utrudnienia. Pasażerów z biletem na pociąg 1234 prosimy o przejście na peron czwarty. Następny pociąg pospieszny do Krakowa odjeżdża o godzinie szesnastej zero zero."

Zaznacz właściwą odpowiedź:

1. Pociąg jedzie z Warszawy do Krakowa.

2. Pociąg odjeżdża z peronu trzeciego.

3. Peron trzeci jest nieczynny z powodu remontu.

4. Następny pociąg do Krakowa odjeżdża o godzinie 15:00.

Klucz odpowiedzi: 1 – Prawda  |  2 – Fałsz (peron czwarty, nie trzeci)  |  3 – Prawda  |  4 – Fałsz (następny pociąg jest o 16:00, nie o 15:00)

Ćwiczenie 2 — Zaznacz właściwą odpowiedź. (A, B lub C)

Instrukcja: Proszę przeczytać zapis rozmowy telefonicznej i wybrać właściwą odpowiedź spośród trzech podanych możliwości (A, B lub C).

Tekst rozmowy (zapis dialogu):

Anna: Halo, dzień dobry. Chciałabym zarezerwować stolik na sobotni wieczór.

Kelner: Dzień dobry, oczywiście. Na ile osób?

Anna: Na cztery osoby. Czy możliwe o godzinie dziewiętnastej?

Kelner: Niestety, o dziewiętnastej mamy już zajęte wszystkie stoliki. Mamy wolne miejsca o osiemnastej lub o dwudziestej.

Anna: W takim razie wezmę o dwudziestej.

Kelner: Dobrze. Rezerwacja na cztery osoby, sobota, godzina dwudziesta. Na jakie nazwisko?

Anna: Kowalska.

Kelner: Dziękujemy. Do zobaczenia w sobotę, pani Kowalska.

Zaznacz właściwą odpowiedź:

1. Na kiedy Anna rezerwuje stolik?

2. Ile osób przyjdzie do restauracji?

3. O której godzinie Anna zarezerwowała stolik?

4. Dlaczego Anna nie mogła zarezerwować stolika na godzinę 19:00?

Klucz odpowiedzi: 1 – C (sobotę)  |  2 – B (cztery osoby)  |  3 – C (20:00)  |  4 – B (wszystkie stoliki zajęte o 19:00)
