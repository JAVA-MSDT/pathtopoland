Subtest 2: Reading (Czytanie)

Three reading tasks, 45 minutes, real Polish text. Comprehension is not about understanding every word — it is about extracting the right information from the right place. This page gives you the structure and strategy to do that reliably.

Subtest 2 of 5 45 Minutes 51% Pass Threshold 3 Task Types

What the Reading Subtest Looks Like

Exam Snapshot — Subtest 2: The Reading subtest runs for 45 minutes and contains 3 reading tasks. Each task presents one or more written text blocks — a news article, an advertisement, an online forum post, a letter, or a magazine feature — followed by comprehension questions. Task formats rotate between matching headings to paragraphs, true/false/not mentioned statements, and multiple choice questions. The language used in texts is standard written Polish at B1 level — complex enough to require focus, but accessible with solid vocabulary and careful reading strategy.

Duration45 minutes (strictly enforced)

The 3 Task Types — One Card Each

Task A: Matching (Dopasowanie)

What you do: You are given a set of short statements, headings, or summaries. You match each one to the paragraph or text it best describes. Each heading is used once; there may be one extra heading as a distractor.

How to approach it

  1. Scan each paragraph for its main topic — read only the first and last sentence of each. These usually contain the topic sentence and the concluding point. Avoid reading the full paragraph until you have a shortlist of matching headings.
  2. Eliminate obviously wrong options first. A heading about technology cannot match a paragraph describing a cultural festival. Cross out clear mismatches before making any positive decisions.
  3. Watch for paraphrase traps. The correct heading will NOT repeat the words used in the paragraph — it will express the same idea with different vocabulary. A heading that shares many exact words with a paragraph is often a carefully placed distractor.
  4. Match by topic, not by shared words. Find the ONE main topic per paragraph — ignore supporting details, examples, and statistics. The heading describes the topic, not a detail.
Timing target: Allocate approximately 12–15 minutes for a matching task. If you are stuck on one paragraph for more than 90 seconds, move on and return at the end. A correct match elsewhere often eliminates the remaining options by elimination.

Task B: True / False / Not Mentioned (Prawda / Fałsz / Brak informacji)

What you do: You are given a series of statements about one or more texts. For each statement, you decide: is it confirmed by the text (prawda), directly contradicted by the text (fałsz), or is the topic simply not addressed at all (brak informacji)?

The critical distinction

⚠️ CRITICAL: "Not mentioned" (brak informacji) means the topic is NOT discussed ANYWHERE in the text — not that the statement is merely incorrect. If the text says something different, the answer is FALSE. Only choose "not mentioned" when the text is completely silent on the subject.
  1. Find the exact sentence in the text. Do not rely on your general knowledge or what you believe to be true about the topic. The only evidence that counts is what is written on the page in front of you.
  2. When hesitating between False and Not Mentioned: ask yourself — does the text say anything at all about this subject? If yes (even if the detail contradicts the statement), the answer is False. If the text never mentions the subject of the statement at all, then it is Not Mentioned.
  3. Mark the location in the text. When you find the sentence that answers a question, mark it lightly with a pencil bracket. This prevents you from re-reading the entire text for every question.
Strategy note: Examiners use synonyms and paraphrase. The statement "The company employs over 500 people" might be confirmed by a text sentence reading "The firm has a workforce of more than half a thousand." Train yourself to match meaning, not matching words.

Task C: Multiple Choice Comprehension (Wielokrotny wybór)

What you do: You read a longer text (typically 4–6 paragraphs) and answer multiple choice questions about it. Each question has 3 options (A, B, C). Only one is fully supported by the text.

How to approach it

  1. Read the questions before the text. Each question contains a key noun — a person's name, a place, a concept. Scan the text for that noun to find the relevant paragraph. You can often answer 60–70% of questions by reading only 2–3 paragraphs.
  2. Locate the relevant paragraph by scanning for key nouns from the question. Polish texts signal paragraph topics with their opening sentences. Scan first sentences only until you find the right section.
  3. The correct answer must be explicitly supported. Do not use inference, general knowledge, or your personal opinions. The correct answer is always a close paraphrase of something directly stated in the text.
  4. Eliminate first. If two options are plausible, re-read the relevant paragraph very carefully. The incorrect options are usually true statements about a different part of the text, or plausible-sounding claims that the text never actually makes.

Reading Strategy — 5 Key Techniques

The 3-Pass Reading Strategy — apply to every task

Use this sequence on every reading task, in every exam you do. It prevents the most common time-management failure — reading the full text first and then searching for answers.

Pass 1 — Questions first (2 minutes): Read every question and answer option before the text. Underline the key content word in each question (a name, a number, a time, a reason). Your brain will search for those patterns automatically while reading.
Pass 2 — Skim for structure (3 minutes): Read only the first and last sentence of each paragraph. This gives you the skeleton of the text and tells you which paragraph is relevant to which question. Do not read full paragraphs yet.
Pass 3 — Targeted reading (remaining time): Read only the paragraphs relevant to your unanswered questions. Do not re-read the entire text from beginning to end. This targeted approach is 3–4 times faster and more accurate than a full re-read.
Tip 4 — Watch for paraphrase. The text will never repeat the exact wording of a question. The examiner always uses synonyms. Practice this: when you find the answer in the text, verify it by confirming the idea matches — not the words.
Tip 5 — Manage time: ~15 minutes per task. With 3 tasks and 45 minutes, budget 15 minutes per task and keep to it. If a question is taking too long, mark it, move on, and return in the final 5 minutes. An unanswered question at the end costs exactly as many points as one you spent 4 minutes debating.

Common Text Topics at B1 Level

What reading passages are about — familiarise yourself with the vocabulary before exam day

B1 reading texts are drawn from a predictable range of topics. The vocabulary within each topic cluster repeats across exams. Spending one study session per topic — reading 2–3 articles on that subject in Polish — dramatically increases the speed at which you can process unfamiliar texts on exam day.

✈️ Travel and Tourism

Descriptions of places, accommodation reviews, travel tips, booking confirmations, tourist guides. Key vocab: nocleg, atrakcja turystyczna, podróż, bilet, rezerwacja, przewodnik.

🏥 Health and Medicine

Medical advice articles, interview with a doctor, hospital notices, healthy lifestyle tips. Key vocab: zdrowie, leczenie, choroba, wizyta lekarska, recepta, dieta.

💼 Work and Career

Job advertisements, workplace interviews, career advice columns, office announcements. Key vocab: praca, zawód, zatrudnienie, wynagrodzenie, umowa, pracodawca.

📱 Technology and Lifestyle

Articles about smartphones, social media, online shopping, modern habits. Key vocab: technologia, internet, urządzenie, aplikacja, codzienne życie, nawyk.

🏰 Polish Culture and Geography

Features on Polish cities, traditions, historical events, cultural festivals, national cuisine. Key vocab: tradycja, kultura, historia, zwyczaj, region, zabytki.

🌿 Environment and Social Issues

Ecology articles, climate discussion, community initiatives, volunteer programmes. Key vocab: środowisko, ochrona, recykling, społeczeństwo, inicjatywa, zmiana klimatu.

Vocabulary Strategies for Reading

How to build reading vocabulary efficiently — not by memorising word lists

Random word lists do not build reading comprehension. These three strategies build the vocabulary needed to decode unfamiliar B1 texts under time pressure.

1. Learn Word Families

Polish is a highly inflected language, and the same root appears across many word forms. Learning the noun, verb, and adjective form of the same root simultaneously triples the value of each vocabulary item you learn. Example:

praca (noun — work/job) pracować (verb — to work) pracowity (adjective — hardworking) pracownik (noun — employee) pracodawca (noun — employer)

When you encounter an unknown word in a text, look for a known root inside it. Nieodpowiedzialny — you may not know this word, but if you know odpowiedź (answer, response) and the prefix nie- (un-/not) and suffix -ny (adjective), you can deduce "irresponsible" without a dictionary.

2. Master Transition Words

Transition words control the logical flow of a text — they tell you whether a sentence is continuing the same idea, contrasting it, or explaining a cause. Matching and multiple choice questions often hinge on correctly interpreting a contrast or causal relationship.

3. Polish Word Order — What to Know for Reading

Unlike English and some other Slavic languages, Polish adjectives come before the noun they modify: ciekawy artykuł (interesting article), duże miasto (big city). The key reading implication: in long noun phrases, the final noun carries the core meaning and the preceding adjectives refine it. Parsing a complex noun phrase: identify the last noun first, then work backwards through the modifiers.

Exception to watch for: Some adjectives that follow a noun carry a specific meaning shift — woda mineralna (mineral water, fixed phrase) versus zimna woda (cold water, descriptive). Fixed phrases with post-nominal adjectives are more common in product names, official titles, and set expressions.

Ćwiczenie — Prawda / Fałsz / Brak informacji

Przeczytaj tekst, a następnie odpowiedz na pytania poniżej.

Tekst:

Warszawa jest największym miastem Polski i jej stolicą od 1596 roku. Miasto leży nad rzeką Wisłą i liczy ponad 1,8 miliona mieszkańców. W ostatnich latach Warszawa stała się jednym z najdynamiczniej rozwijających się miast w Europie Środkowej, szczególnie w sektorze technologicznym. Wiele międzynarodowych firm otworzyło tu swoje biura regionalne. Miasto słynie również z bogatej kultury i licznych muzeów, w tym Muzeum Powstania Warszawskiego, które odwiedza rocznie ponad 500 tysięcy turystów.

Zdecyduj: czy każde zdanie jest Prawda, Fałsz czy Brak informacji?

1. Warszawa jest stolicą Polski od XVI wieku.

Prawda. Tekst podaje „od 1596 roku" — rok 1596 należy do XVI wieku. Zdanie jest bezpośrednio potwierdzone w tekście.

2. Warszawa jest drugim co do wielkości miastem w Europie Środkowej pod względem liczby mieszkańców.

Brak informacji. Tekst podaje liczbę mieszkańców Warszawy i informuje, że jest największym miastem w Polsce, ale nie porównuje jej z innymi miastami Europy Środkowej. Ranking Warszawy wśród miast Europy Środkowej nie jest w tekście poruszony.

3. Muzeum Powstania Warszawskiego odwiedza rocznie ponad pół miliona turystów.

Prawda. Tekst podaje „ponad 500 tysięcy turystów" rocznie. „Ponad pół miliona" to ponad 500 000 — zdanie jest parafrazą bezpośrednio potwierdzoną w tekście.

4. W ostatnich latach międzynarodowe firmy przeniosły swoje siedziby z Warszawy do innych miast.

Fałsz. Tekst stwierdza coś odwrotnego: „Wiele międzynarodowych firm otworzyło tu swoje biura regionalne" — wiele firm otworzyło biura w Warszawie. Zdanie przeczy temu, co mówi tekst, dlatego odpowiedź to Fałsz — a nie Brak informacji.
