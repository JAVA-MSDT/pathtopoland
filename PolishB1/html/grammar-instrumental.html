Step 1: Narzędnik (Instrumental Case)

The most predictable case in Polish — master it first. It forms the backbone of every self-introduction and is the entry point for the entire case system.

Step 1 of 7 Easiest Case Grammar Subtest Start Here

Why Learn This Case First?

Two reasons it beats every other case as a starting point

1. Highly predictable endings. Unlike Genitive, which has two competing endings for masculine nouns (-a vs. -u) that require memorisation word-by-word, Instrumental endings follow a near-perfect rule: masculine and neuter nouns always take -em, feminine nouns always take -ą, and plurals always take -ami. You can apply the rule to any new word immediately without looking it up.

2. It forms the baseline for introductions. The very first thing an examiner will ask in the Speaking subtest is what your profession is — "Kim Pan/Pani jest z zawodu?" The answer uses Instrumental. If you get this right from the start, you build confidence and avoid the most common speaking error (using the Nominative form instead).

⚠️ The single most penalised mistake in the Speaking subtest: saying "Jestem lekarka" (Nominative) instead of "Jestem lekarką" (Instrumental). One rule, taught first, eliminates this error entirely.

When to Use the Instrumental Case

Three main triggers — memorise these first

Trigger 1 być + profession / nationality
→
Trigger 2 interesować się (to be interested in)
→
Trigger 3 z (with) + accompanying noun

Trigger 1 — być + Profession or Nationality

Whenever you say what someone is (by profession, role, or nationality), the complement noun takes Instrumental. The trigger word is any form of być: jest, jestem, jesteś, będę…

  • Jestem programistą. — I am a programmer.
  • Ona jest lekarką. — She is a doctor.
  • On jest Polakiem. — He is Polish.
  • Będę inżynierem. — I will be an engineer.

Trigger 2 — interesować się (to be interested in)

The verb interesować się always governs the Instrumental case. The noun that follows — your area of interest — changes to its Instrumental form.

  • Interesuję się sportem. — I am interested in sport.
  • Ona interesuje się muzyką. — She is interested in music.
  • Interesuję się historią Polski. — I am interested in Polish history.
  • Czy interesujesz się polityką? — Are you interested in politics?

Trigger 3 — z (with) + Accompanying Noun

The preposition z meaning "with" (accompaniment or ingredient) always takes Instrumental. This is distinct from z meaning "from", which takes Genitive.

  • Kawa z mlekiem. — Coffee with milk.
  • Idę z kolegą. — I am going with a colleague.
  • Rozmawiałam z szefem. — I talked with the boss.
  • Wyszedłem z psem. — I went out with the dog.

Ending Rules — The Complete Picture

Instrumental endings at a glance

These endings apply to all nouns and adjectives. Memorise the pattern, not individual words.

Category Ending Base form → Instrumental Notes
Masculine Inanimate ♂📦
(object)		 -em dom → domem · komputer → komputerem After soft consonants: -iem (e.g. gość → gościem)
Masculine Animate ♂🧑
(person / animal)		 -em
Same as inanimate!		 brat → bratem · lekarz → lekarzem · pies → psem Animacy makes no difference in Instrumental — both animate and inanimate take -em
Feminine singular -ą kobieta → kobietą · lekarka → lekarką Replaces the final -a or -i/-ia of the noun
Neuter singular -em miasto → miastem · dziecko → dzieckiem Same ending as masculine
Plural (all genders) -ami znajomi → znajomymi · dzieci → dziećmi A few animate nouns use -mi instead (e.g. ludźmi)
Adjectives (masc. / neuter) -ym / -im dobry → dobrym · tani → tanim Use -im after k, g, or soft consonants
Adjectives (feminine) -ą dobra → dobrą · tania → tanią Adjective and noun share the -ą ending
Quick test: To check whether a form is correct, ask "With what? / By whom?" — if that question fits the meaning, use Instrumental. Example: "She is a doctor" → ask "She is what?" → Instrumental. "Coffee with milk" → "with what?" → Instrumental.

Adjective Agreement in Instrumental (Zgodność Przymiotnika)

How adjectives change — gender agreement in Instrumental

In the Instrumental case, adjectives must agree with the noun's gender. Critically: animacy does NOT affect adjective endings here — masculine animate nouns (people, animals) and masculine inanimate nouns (objects) use the exact same adjective form.

⭐ Animacy rule in Instrumental: Animacy only splits adjective forms in the Accusative case. In Instrumental (and Genitive, Dative, Locative), masculine animate and inanimate nouns share the same adjective ending. Only gender matters here.

Masculine Animate ♂🧑
(person / animal)		 -ym / -im
Same as inanimate!		 dobry student dobrym studentem Jestem dobrym studentem.
Feminine ♀ -ą / -ią dobra studentka dobrą studentką Ona jest dobrą studentką.
Neuter ⬜ -ym / -im
(same as masculine)		 dobre dziecko dobrym dzieckiem On jest dobrym dzieckiem.
Plural (all genders) -ymi / -imi dobrzy przyjaciele dobrymi przyjaciółmi Idę z dobrymi przyjaciółmi.
When to use -ym vs -im: Use -im after stems ending in k or g: drogi → drogim, tani → tanim (soft stems). Use -ym after all other hard consonant stems: dobry → dobrym, nowy → nowym, duży → dużym. The feminine adjective always takes -ą, matching the noun's Instrumental ending.
Quick contrast — same adjective, different genders:
"Jestem dobrym programistą." — masculine (Instrumental -ym)
"Ona jest dobrą programistką." — feminine (Instrumental -ą)
"On jest dobrym dzieckiem." — neuter (Instrumental -ym, same as masculine)
"Idę z nowym kolegą." — masculine animate with z (Instrumental -ym; animacy makes no difference here)

Time Expressions — Instrumental Without a Preposition

Exam Snapshots

Exam Snapshot — Speaking Task: A very common oral prompt is: "Proszę powiedzieć, kim Pan/Pani jest z zawodu." (Please say what your profession is.) You must answer in Instrumental: "Jestem inżynierem / jestem nauczycielką / jestem studentem." Writing the Nominative form ("Jestem inżynier") is a grammar error that costs points in both the Grammar and Speaking subtests.
Exam Snapshot — Grammar Fill-in: "Ona jest ______ (lekarka)." → Correct answer: lekarką (feminine Instrumental). The most common trap: writing lekarka (Nominative). The trigger word is always the verb jest / być followed by a profession or nationality noun. Spot the verb → switch to Instrumental.

Ćwiczenia

✏️ Ćwiczenie — Narzędnik

Wpisz słowo w nawiasie w formie narzędnika. Spróbuj odpowiedzieć przed sprawdzeniem.

1. Ona jest ______ (lekarka).

lekarką — Feminine noun, ending -ą. lekarka → lekarką. Triggered by jest (być).

2. Idę do kina z ______ (kolega).

kolegą — Masculine noun ending in -a (like feminine), Instrumental -ą. Triggered by preposition z (with).

3. Interesuję się ______ (sport).

sportem — Masculine noun, ending -em. sport → sportem. Triggered by interesować się.

4. Jestem ______ (programista).

programistą — Masculine noun ending in -a, Instrumental -ą. programista → programistą. Triggered by jestem (być).

5. Pijemy kawę ______ (mleko).

z mlekiem — Neuter noun, ending -em. mleko → mlekiem. Triggered by preposition z (with). Full phrase: kawa z mlekiem.
