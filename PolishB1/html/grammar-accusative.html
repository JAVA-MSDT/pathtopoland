Step 2: Biernik (Accusative Case)

The direct object case — marks what a verb acts upon. The accusative introduces the most important concept in Polish grammar: animacy. Whether a masculine noun is animate (a person or animal) or inanimate (an object) determines which ending it takes and which adjective form to use.

Step 2 of 7 Direct Object Animacy Split Grammar Subtest ★
⭐ EXAM PRIORITY: The Accusative is the default object case — most transitive verbs use it. Knowing when Accusative turns into Genitive (after negation) is one of the most tested transformations on the B1 exam.

What Is the Accusative Case?

The case that marks the direct object

The Accusative (Biernik) answers the question Kogo? Co? (Whom? What?). It marks the person or thing that is directly acted upon by the verb.

  • Widzę dom. — I see a house. (dom is what is seen)
  • Kupuję kawę. — I'm buying coffee. (kawę is what is bought)
  • Kocham mamę. — I love mum. (mamę is whom is loved)
  • Znam tego lekarza. — I know this doctor. (lekarza is who is known)
Quick test: Ask "What/Whom does the subject do this to?" — the answer is in Accusative. "Widzę ____?" → dom. If the verb is negated (nie + verb), the Accusative object shifts to Genitive — see the negation section below.

The Animacy Split — The Most Important Rule

🔑 KEY CONCEPT: Animacy only matters for Masculine Singular nouns in the Accusative.
  • Masculine Inanimate (objects, things): Accusative = same as Nominative — no change!
  • Masculine Animate (people, animals): Accusative = same as Genitive — adds -a!
  • Feminine, Neuter, and Plural follow their own consistent rules (see the table below).

Adjective Agreement in Accusative (Zgodność Przymiotnika)

How adjectives change — gender and animacy agreement in Accusative

In the Accusative case, adjectives must agree with both the gender and the animacy of the noun. This is the only case where animacy splits adjective forms — masculine animate nouns (people, animals) get a different ending from masculine inanimate nouns (objects).

⭐ Animacy rule in Accusative — the only case where it splits adjectives: Masculine Animate uses -ego/-iego (same form as Genitive). Masculine Inanimate uses -y/-i (same form as Nominative — no change). In every other case (Instrumental, Genitive, Dative, Locative), masculine animate and inanimate share the same adjective ending.

Feminine ♀ -ą / -ią

Neuter ⬜ -e / -ie

Pattern summary: Masculine Animate follows Genitive (noun brat → brata and adjective dobry → dobrego). Masculine Inanimate and Neuter stay at Nominative — nothing changes. Feminine is unique: noun -a → -ę, adjective -a → -ą.
Quick contrast — same adjective, animacy changes everything:
"Lubię dobry komputer." — masculine inanimate (adj stays -y; no change)
"Lubię dobrego kota." — masculine animate (adj changes to -ego)
"Lubię dobrą kawę." — feminine (adj changes to -ą; noun -a → -ę)
"Lubię dobre miasto." — neuter (adj stays -e; no change)

Four patterns to memorise:
  • Masculine Animate: Accusative = Genitive (noun AND adjective change the same way). "Animate behaves like Genitive."
  • Masculine Inanimate: Accusative = Nominative (nothing changes). "Objects don't change in Accusative."
  • Neuter: Accusative = Nominative (nothing changes). "Neuter never changes in Accusative."
  • Feminine: Accusative is unique — noun -a → -ę, adjective -a → -ą. Different from both Nominative and Genitive.

Critical distinction — słuchać vs słyszeć:
Słucham muzyki — I listen to music (Genitive — słuchać governs Genitive).
Słyszę muzykę — I hear music (Accusative — słyszeć governs Accusative).
This pair is frequently tested because they look similar but require different cases.

Accusative Prepositions

Prepositions that take Accusative — especially important for direction

Several prepositions govern Accusative. Many of them express direction/movement towards something, contrasting with Locative which expresses being at rest in a place.

Preposition Meaning (Accusative) Example (Accusative) Contrast — Rest (Locative / Instrumental)
na onto / to (direction) Idę na pocztę. Jestem na poczcie. — Locative
przez through / for (duration) Jadę przez miasto.
Spałem przez dwie godziny.		 No Locative pair — always Accusative
za in exchange for / (direction) behind Zapłacił za bilet.
Poszedł za dom.		 Schował się za domem. — Instrumental
pod under (direction) Kot wszedł pod stół. Kot siedzi pod stołem. — Instrumental
w into (direction) Weszła w las. Jest w lesie. — Locative
o by (time increment) Spóźnił się o godzinę. Myślę o tobie. — Locative
The na rule — direction vs. location:
na + Accusative = direction (going to): Idę na pocztę, Idę na studia, Jadę na urlop.
na + Locative = location (being at): Jestem na poczcie, Studiuję na uczelni, Jestem na urlopie.

Negation Rule — Accusative Turns into Genitive

When you negate a verb, its Accusative object shifts to Genitive

This is one of the most reliably tested rules on the B1 exam. When nie negates a transitive verb, the direct object automatically changes from Accusative to Genitive.

Positive sentence → Accusative. Negated sentence → Genitive. Always — no exceptions.
Positive (Accusative) Negative (Genitive) What Changed
Mam samochód.Nie mam samochodu.samochód → samochodu (masculine inanim. → Genitive -u)
Piję kawę.Nie piję kawy.kawę → kawy (feminine Accusative → Genitive)
Widzę brata.Nie widzę brata.Looks the same! Masculine animate Acc. = Gen., so no visible change
Kupuję bilet.Nie kupuję biletu.bilet → biletu (masculine inanim. takes Genitive -u)
Lubię kawę.Nie lubię kawy.kawę → kawy
Oglądam film.Nie oglądam filmu.film → filmu (masculine inanim. -u)
Interesting case — masculine animate: Widzę brata (Accusative = Genitive form) → Nie widzę brata (Genitive). The word doesn't change visually because masculine animate Accusative already looks like Genitive. The case technically shifts, but the form is the same.

Ćwiczenia

✏️ Ćwiczenie — Biernik

Wpisz właściwą formę biernika słowa w nawiasie. Spróbuj odpowiedzieć przed sprawdzeniem.

1. Widzę ______ (dom).

dom — Masculine Inanimate → Accusative = Nominative. dom → dom. No change! Sentence: Widzę dom.

2. Znam ______ (ten lekarz).

tego lekarza — Masculine Animate → Accusative = Genitive. Noun: lekarz → lekarza. Demonstrative adjective: ten → tego. Full sentence: Znam tego lekarza.

3. Kupuję ______ (świeża kawa).

świeżą kawę — Feminine noun, -a → -ę: kawa → kawę. Adjective feminine Accusative: świeża → świeżą. Sentence: Kupuję świeżą kawę.

4. Oglądam ______ (nowy film).

nowy film — Masculine Inanimate → Accusative = Nominative. Noun film → film (no change). Adjective nowy → nowy (no change). Sentence: Oglądam nowy film.

5. Mam ______ (dobry pies).

dobrego psa — Masculine Animate (animal) → Accusative = Genitive. Noun: pies → psa (stem change: ie → Ø). Adjective: dobry → dobrego. Sentence: Mam dobrego psa.

6. Lubię ______ (duże miasto).

duże miasto — Neuter → Accusative = Nominative. miasto → miasto (no change). duże → duże (no change). Sentence: Lubię duże miasto.

7. Nie widzę ______ (samochód). (negation!)

samochodu — Negation forces Genitive! Masculine noun, Genitive ending -u. samochód → samochodu. Sentence: Nie widzę samochodu.

8. Lubię ______ (dobry kot) ale nie lubię ______ (duży pająk).

dobrego kota and dużego pająka — Both are Masculine Animate (animals) → Accusative = Genitive. kot → kota (adds -a), adjective dobry → dobrego. pająk → pająka, adjective duży → dużego. Full: Lubię dobrego kota ale nie lubię dużego pająka.
